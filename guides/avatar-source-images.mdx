An avatar builds from a single still: a real person, an animated or
generated character, a mascot, or something synthetic. What decides
whether the build succeeds is not realism but whether face detection can
find a complete set of features.

## Requirements

Upload a PNG or JPEG meeting all of the following.

- One face in the frame, with nothing else competing for attention.
- A face or face-like structure looking directly at the camera, with both
  eyes visible.
- A distinct nose or nose bridge, and a visible mouth, chin, cheeks, and
  forehead.
- A face large enough to inspect, with room around the head so nothing is
  cut off.
- Even lighting across the face.
- A clean image, without UI, stickers, text, or a watermark over it.

Stylized, mascot, and synthetic subjects qualify on the same terms: two
eyes, a nose area, a mouth, a chin or jaw, and a head near the middle of
the frame.

## By subject

Each kind of subject meets those requirements differently.

### Real people

Use a head-and-shoulders portrait with the person centered and looking
forward. The eyes, nose, mouth, chin, and forehead should all be visible,
and a neutral or slightly smiling expression works best. Avoid sunglasses,
masks, hands on the face, heavy hair over the eyes, strong backlight,
motion blur, and tight crops.

### Animated or generated characters

Animated and AI-generated characters work well when they are composed like
a portrait photo. Use one character, front-facing and centered, with clear
eyes, nose, mouth, chin, forehead, and a symmetrical head shape. Avoid
action scenes, crowd shots, partial faces, dramatic camera angles, and
props covering the face.

### Animal or mascot characters

Animal and mascot avatars work best as a human-like mascot portrait rather
than a normal pet photo. Use a front-facing character with large visible
eyes, a centered snout or nose, a clear mouth, cheeks, a chin or jaw
shape, and a head-and-shoulders crop. Avoid full-body animals, side views,
tiny faces, busy outdoor backgrounds, and pets holding toys in their
mouth.

### Synthetic characters

Synthetic characters work best with a human-like or mascot-like face
layout. Use a character with two visible eyes, a nose bridge or nose-like
shape, a mouth, chin, forehead, and a symmetrical head. Avoid screen-only
faces, black visors, helmets with no visible facial structure, reflective
masks, and abstract heads.

## If a build fails

An avatar at `status: "failed"` carries the reason on `failure_reason`.
When no face was detected, the fix is usually a clearer front-facing
source with stronger facial structure, not a different subject.

For stylized, mascot, and synthetic characters, make each attempt more
portrait-like: visible eyes, a nose area, a mouth, a chin, and a
forehead, with nothing hiding or competing with the face.

When an image qualifies, upload it with
[Create an avatar](/guides/avatars#create-an-avatar).
